# Draft angles

## Draft A
Strongest thesis: the Atlas's biggest gains are in honesty and preparedness, not new features. Fix scenario defaults that understate hazard (rock-site Vs30, single GMPE, slow constant flood speeds, list-only lakes), make ranges and 'hypothetical, not a warning' framing the main display, and position the site as a pre-event planning tool beside official warnings.

## Draft B
Steelman contrarian: the preparedness-only consensus undersells the Atlas. Argue that official warnings in Nepal still fail at speed, specificity and language, that global open flood forecasts, CAP, BIPAD and landslide data make a near-real-time complement feasible, and that simple transparent models are good enough if their spread is shown; test where this view breaks.

## Draft C
Synthesis and practitioner roadmap: organise around concrete, buildable changes with boundary conditions (when simple models suffice, when a daily copy is enough, when expansion needs safeguards). Weigh each change by evidence grade, delivery cost on low bandwidth, and maintenance burden for a small volunteer project; end with a sequenced roadmap including validation tasks.
