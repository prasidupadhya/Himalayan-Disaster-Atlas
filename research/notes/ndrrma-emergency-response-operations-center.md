BHOTEKOSHI FLOOD EMERGENCY RESPONSE
REPORT
SITUATION REPORT # 06 — COMPREHENSIVE INFORMAL TRANSLATION & UPDATE
1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
Disaster Event: On August 26, 2026 (Bhadra 10, 2083) at 08:40 AM, a massive landslide and avalanche
covering approximately 1 sq. km collapsed on the upper Tibet catchment area of the Lhende Khola (approx. 20
km northeast of the Rasuwagadhi border point). This colossal slide registered an M 5.2 equivalent seismic
signal and immediately triggered a catastrophic debris-laden outburst flood. The flood surged through
Rasuwagadhi, Timure, and Syabrubesi, entering the Trishuli River system and creating extreme,
life-threatening risk across riparian corridors in Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha, and Chitwan districts.
Urgent Residual Threat (Blocked River): Satellite imagery analyzed by the National Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) on August 27, 2026 (Bhadra 11, 2083) at 11:44 AM
revealed a highly critical development: the main channel of the Lhende Khola is blocked about 18 km
upstream from the Rasuwagadhi border post. A massive reservoir/lake covering approximately 0.11 sq. km
has formed behind this temporary natural landslide dam. There is an imminent risk of a secondary,
catastrophic Outburst Flood if this natural dam breaches. Downstream settlements across all five districts
remain on maximum high-alert, and immediate relocation of riverbank communities to safe high ground
continues.
2. HUMANITARIAN IMPACT: CASUALTIES AND INJURIES
The human toll of the debris flood has risen drastically as downstream search-and-recovery operations
proceed. As of 10:00 PM on August 27, 2026, the cumulative casualties and out-of-contact persons are as
follows:
CRITICAL HUMANITARIAN METRICS (AS OF BHADRA 11, 10:00 PM):
• Recovered Deceased: 359 dead bodies recovered (identification pending across 8 districts).
• Missing / Out-of-Contact: 977 individuals registered missing, including 517 foreign tourists.
• Injured Survivors: 130 survivors registered for hospital treatment; 67 remain actively hospitalized.
Source: National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) SITREP

3. SYSTEMIC COORDINATION AND EMERGENCY RESPONSE
The NDRRMA has deployed a highly structured multi-agency mobilization strategy to tackle the scale of this
crisis. A total of 13,295 security personnel are deployed in ground search, rescue, and security operations,
supported by an aerial fleet of 15 helicopters (6 military and 9 private sector). To ensure specialized focus, 12
thematic emergency coordination teams have been formed and are fully active in the field:
•
Search, Rescue & Evacuation Team: Led by Nepal Army, Nepal Police, and APF. Mobilized 13,295 officers;
rescued 1,552 citizens to date.
•
Emergency Health & Medical Response Team: Led by Ministry of Health and Population. Deployed army
medical units & doctors to Nuwakot.
•
Dead Body Management Team: Jointly managed by Health, Home Affairs, Foreign Affairs, and APF to handle
dignified identification & storage.
•
International Assistance & Donor Team: Led by Ministry of Finance & Foreign Affairs to coordinate with
international relief agencies.
•
Transport & Road Connectivity Team: Deploying heavy machinery to restore highways and clear roadblocks.
•
Rapid Damage & Needs Assessment Team: Conducting rapid surveys to map out damaged infrastructure and
household needs.
•
Missing Persons & Family Assistance Team: Managing missing persons databases, supporting families, and
publicizing survivor registries.
•
Relief Distribution Team: Ensuring prompt supply of dry food, clean water, tents, and medical packs to
displaced families.
•
Airport & Air Operations Team: Coordinating airspace and landing zones for the 15 rescue and supply
helicopters.

4. INFRASTRUCTURE IMPACT & ROAD RECOVERY
The physical destruction of regional corridors is severe, completely cutting off major portions of Rasuwa district.
Confirmed infrastructure losses include 35 motorable bridges, 45 suspension bridges, and over 40
kilometers of paved roadways.
Restoration Progress: The major roadblock at Devighat has been fully cleared and reopened to search and
rescue traffic. Active recovery teams are currently operating heavy equipment (loaders, excavators, backhoes)
to clear thick mud and debris across the Dhunche-Grang sector, Vidur-Trishuli-Dunge sector, and the
Baireni-Malekhu section. The Road Division Office is preparing to launch emergency Bailey bridges to
re-establish vital transit links.
5. CABINET DIRECTIVES & GOVERNMENT FINANCIAL AID
Following an emergency meeting of the Council of Ministers, the Government of Nepal formally declared the
affected local levels across Rasuwa, Nuwakot, and Dhading as Disaster Crisis Zones for a duration of three
months.
To enable immediate, localized response, the Cabinet released NPR 2.5 Crore (NPR 25,000,000) in cash relief
directly to the District Disaster Management Committees, distributed as: NPR 1 Crore to Rasuwa, NPR 1 Crore
to Nuwakot, and NPR 50 Lakh to Dhading. All government hospitals, the Nepali Army, and regional clinics have
been ordered to provide free, comprehensive healthcare to all flood survivors. The Ministry of Finance is
coordinating supplementary reconstruction funding.
