Abstract
The accelerating retreat of glaciers under climate change is driving the rapid expansion of glacial lakes across High Mountain Asia, progressively increasing the frequency and magnitude of glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs) and the risk they pose to downstream communities. Despite this, conventional hazard assessment frameworks remain predominantly focused on larger lakes, leaving smaller yet potentially dangerous water bodies systematically underrepresented. This study investigates the 2024 Thame GLOF event in the Khumbu region, Nepal, using multi-temporal satellite remote sensing to reconstruct long-term glacial lake evolution, outburst dynamics, and downstream impact across five adjacent lakes within a single catchment. We investigated the cascading breach mechanism whereby GL5, underwent dam failure in 2024 and triggered a secondary breach of the hydrologically connected GL4, generating a compound outburst substantially exceeding the destructive potential of either lake independently. Prior to the GLOF event, GL5 expanded more than 12 times its area from approximately 0.008 km² to 0.1 km² at an average annual growth rate of 18% between 2015 and 2024. This represents one of the few directly observed cascading multi-lake GLOF events documented in HMA. The flood impacted approximately 130 Ha of the catchment, reached a maximum inundation width of ~ 400 m at Thame village, destroying 36 households and 10 Ha of farmland, and resulting in an estimated economic loss of 4.13 million USD. Our study underscores the critical inadequacy of size-based lake prioritisation in GLOF hazard frameworks and advocate that consequence-based, catchment-scale assessments explicitly incorporating downstream geological conditions, community exposure, and socio-economic vulnerability are essential. Finally, we recommend that future GLOF hazard assessments adopt localised approaches that can incorporate potentially dangerous smaller lakes and the produced scientific findings to be actively translated into proper implementations safeguarding vulnerable mountain communities.
